1. Master's Thesis

Title: On the k-numerical range of cyclic shift matrices 

Field: Linear Algebra

Year: 2025

Abstract: 

In 2023, M.-T. Chien, S. Kirkland, C.-K. Li, and H. Nakazato conjecturedthat the cyclic shift S(a1, a3, a5, . . . , a6, a4, a2) with 0 ≤ a1 ≤ · · · ≤ an has thelargest numerical range among all cyclic shift matrices with the same collectionof weights. H.-L. Gau proved the conjecture in 2024. In this study, a version ofthe problem for the k-numerical range is formulated. Aspects of the conjectureare explored in this new setting. For small n (at least for n = 2, 3, 4), a necessaryand sufficient condition is established for an n-by-n cyclic shift to have the largestk-numerical range among all n-by-n cyclic shift matrices with the same collectionof nonnegative real weights. The conjecture is proved for n-by-n cyclic shift matrices. The main results are extended to n-by-n cyclic shift matrices whose weightsare complex. In order to prove the main results, properties of the k-numericalrange are revisited. In particular, it is shown that the k-numerical range is theintersection of certain closed half-planes.

2. Bachelor's Thesis

Title: Schur's triangularization theorem on dual complex matrices

Field: Linear Algebra

Year: 2023

Abstract: 

Dual complex numbers are numbers of the form ast + aIε where ast (called thestandard part) and aI (called the infinitesimal part) are complex numbers andε is the dual unit satisfying the following properties: ε ≠ 0 and ε^2 = 0. Dualcomplex matrices are matrices where all of its entries are dual complex numbers.One of the standard results in matrix analysis is Schur’s triangularization theoremwhich states that a square matrix over C is unitarily similar to a triangular matrixwhose diagonal entries are its eigenvalues. We show that Schur’s triangularizationtheorem holds for n × n dual complex matrices whose standard part has n distinct eigenvalues. Some consequences of Schur’s triangularization theorem are alsoexamined, namely results about the trace and the determinant of dual complexmatrices. Moreover, we also show that Sylvester’s theorem on matrix equationscan be extended to dual complex matrices, that is, AX − XB = C has a uniquesolution X if and only if the standard parts of A and B have no eigenvalues incommon.
